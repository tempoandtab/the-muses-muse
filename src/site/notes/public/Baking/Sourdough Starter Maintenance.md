#baking #sourdough 

# Naming Your Starter *(Mine is Bob)*
This is crucial.  My starter's name is ==Robert Breadford==, aka ==Bob==. All starter is referred to as Bob.

# Mother Bob - *Your main starter*
This is your main starter. It stays in the fridge, always hibernating.
## Mother's Weekly Feeding (1:1:1)
1. Take Mother Bob out of the fridge. 
	- *You don't have to let her come to room temp, but I like to leave her out for 30min before feeding to let her wake up a bit.*
2. Feed Mother
	1. **Discard down to 50g.**   Pour all but 50g of Mother into your discard jar
		- *<span class="ftc-color-builtin-cyan" style="color: var(--color-cyan, oklch(from color-mix(in oklch, hsl(178, 100%, 40%) 10%, rgb(63, 186, 182)) min(l, 0.82) min(c, 0.12) h))">Keep a discard jar in the fridge. You can use the discard for many recipes. Often you can substitute discard for active starter (like for cookies, flatbread, etc), and many recipes call for discard specifically, rather than active starter (foccaccia for example).</span>*
	2. **Add 50g Flour**.   Pour in 50g flour (I like bread flour, but all purpose flour is equally fine)
	3. **Add 50g Water**.  Pour in 50g warm water.
	4. **Stir, Stir, Stir.** Stir it all up.
	5. **Let it sit** out on the counter for about an hour. 
		- You want to let it just start to activate, meaning:  let it sit until it starts to make bubbles, then put it right back into the fridge.
	6. Put back in fridge

# Bob Jr. - *For baking*

> [!info| gray compact] Just some explaination (skip this if you want)
> When you're ready to bake, you'll scoop some of Mother Bob out into it's own jar. Mother Bob always stays in the fridge. You'll make Bob Jrs as you need them for baking, and they'll stay out of the fridge.
> Sometimes a recipe will call for a different hydration ratio (not important to know what this means) so making a Baby Bob allows Mother Bob to keep the 1:1:1 ratio, while also allowing you to tweak the ratio for specific bakes.

1. **Make A Baby**.  Scoop 10g of Mother into its own jar.
2. **Feed The Baby**:  
	1. **Add flour and water**, mix ([[public/Baking/Sourdough Starter Maintenance#Junior's Ratios & Time To Peak\|see ratios below]])
	2. **Give Junior a Tutu**.  put a single coffee filter over Junior's jar instead of a lid, and put a rubber band to hold it on (or one of those topless mason jar lids). This lets Junior breathe.
3. **Wait for Junior to Peak**. Use Junior when he's at his peak.
	- An active starter will double or triple in size after you feed it. When the starter reaches its height it's at its *Peak.*
	- The amount of time it takes to peak depends on the feeding ratio ([[public/Baking/Sourdough Starter Maintenance#Junior's Ratios & Time To Peak\|see below]])
4. When Junior's at his peak, you're ready to make dough.
	- Each recipe will tell you how much starter to use to make the dough.
## Junior's Ratios & Time To Peak
You use the ratio of starter to flour to water to control when it peaks.

Example weights use a 10g seed; if you scale the seed up, keep the ratio.

| Ratio   | Starter | Flour | Water | Total | Time to peak | Good for                                                    |
| ------- | ------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1:1:1   | 10g     | 10g   | 10g   | 30g   | 4–6 hrs      | Quick same-day bake, maintenance feeds                      |
| 1:2:2   | 10g     | 20g   | 20g   | 50g   | 6–8 hrs      | Morning feed → afternoon bake                               |
| 1:3:3   | 10g     | 30g   | 30g   | 70g   | 7–10 hrs     | Half-day builds                                             |
| 1:5:5   | 10g     | 50g   | 50g   | 110g  | 8–12 hrs     | Feed before work → peak when home ✅                         |
| 1:8:8   | 10g     | 80g   | 80g   | 170g  | 11–14 hrs    | Long workdays                                               |
| 1:10:10 | 10g     | 100g  | 100g  | 210g  | 12–16 hrs    | Evening feed → morning bake ✅ (14–16 hrs on a cool counter) |
