#sourdough #sourdough/active #bread 

A boule is a traditional, round loaf of bread.
# Baking Prep 
The schedule I'll put here is planned out to have fresh bread saturday morning.
### Make a Bob Jr. (Thursday Night)
I'll usually do this on Thursday night. I'll give it a 1:1:1 feed Thursday night, then a large feeding Friday morning so that it peaks right around when I get back from work.
### Feed Junior (Friday Morning)
[[public/Baking/Sourdough Starter Maintenance#Junior's Ratios & Time To Peak\|See ratios here]]. For me this means a 1:5:5 feeding.
### Do a Float Test on Junior (Friday Afternoon)
- Fill cup with room temp water
- Spoon out a bit of Bob into the cup
- Pass = Bob floats like a little raft: → go to next phase
- Fail: let Junior rest some more until he peaks.

## Mixing & Gluten Building (Friday Afternoon)
*Start right after float test*

- **1. Autolyse (The Soak):**
    - In a large bowl, combine **350g of warm water** and **100g of active Junior, stirring until milky.
    - Add **500g of unbleached bread flour** and mix with your hands or a spatula just until a shaggy dough forms and no dry flour remains.
    - Cover the bowl with a towel and let it rest for **45 minutes**.
	    - Linen or smooth cotton towel works best—avoid fluffy terrycloth towels because the dough will stick to the loops
    
- **2. Incorporate Salt & Stretch and Folds:**
    - Sprinkle **10g of fine sea salt** evenly over the top of the dough, plus a **tiny splash of water (about 1 teaspoon)** to help it dissolve. Pinch and fold the dough to work it in thoroughly.
    - Over the next **2 hours**, perform a **stretch and fold** every **30 minutes** (4 sets total). Grab the underside, stretch it gently upward, and fold it over the top. Rotate the bowl a quarter turn and repeat.
	    - Wet hands a bit
	    - Pinch with full hand/fist on the side and  fold bottom over the top. Do this until all the salt is incorporated, prob ~3 minutes.
	    - **Cover between folds with the towel.**
    
- **3. Bulk Fermentation (Main Rise):** 
	- Cover the bowl and leave to rest in warm spot on your counter. You aren't looking for a sauna or a proofer—just normal room temperature (around 70°F to 75°F is the sweet spot).
	- **3-4 hours later:** 
	    - _…Done when:_ The dough increases in volume by 50% to 75%, looks slightly domed, has a few visible surface bubbles, and jiggles gently when you shake the bowl.
        
- **4. Shaping:**
    - Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface.
	    - "Turning the dough out" just means gently tipping your bulk fermentation bowl upside down so the dough releases and falls out onto your work surface. Let gravity do most of the work—don't aggressively scrape or yank it, or you'll deflate all those lovely gas bubbles Bob worked hard to make.
    - Shape it into a tight round by pulling and folding the edges toward the center, flip it over, and round it against the counter to build surface tension.
	    - Lightly dusted with flour
	    -  **What "tight" means:** When you pull and fold the edges of the dough toward the center, you are creating tension on the outer "skin" of the dough. Think of it like making a tight ball of pizza dough—you want it to hold its shape and feel smooth and taut on top rather than slumping into a flat pancake.
    - Let it rest uncovered for **20 minutes**. Then, give it a final tighten-up shape, flip it upside down into a floured towel-lined bowl or basket, and cover it.
	    - Shape = circle (boule) - bc the dutch oven is circular, not ovular.
	    - Final tighten-up = same shaping motion, but just a quick one
	    - **Towel & flour:** Put a clean kitchen towel (linen or smooth cotton works best—avoid fluffy terrycloth towels because the dough will stick to the loops) inside the bowl. Take a couple of tablespoons of flour (regular flour or rice flour if you have it) and rub it all over the surface of the towel where the dough will sit so it acts as a non-stick barrier.
		- **Covering it:** Once your shaped dough is sitting upside down inside that floured towel, pull the excess corners of the towel up and over the top of the dough to tuck it in, _or_ place a plastic bag over the whole bowl. This keeps the dough from drying out in the fridge.
		- **Direct Plastic Wrap Method:**
			*if using plastic wrap instead of towel*
			1. Dust the Plastic: Lay a large sheet of plastic wrap down on your counter. Lightly dust the top surface of the plastic wrap with a little bit of flour (or rice flour) so the sticky dough won't weld itself to it.
			2. Seam side down into the plastic wrap
			3. Wrap It Up: Bring the corners of the plastic wrap up and gather them loosely over the top of the dough. You want it snug enough to support the dough's shape, but leave just a little bit of slack so the dough can expand slightly as it chills.
			4. Place in the Bowl: Carefully pick up the wrapped bundle by the gathered plastic and drop it right-side down (or seam-side up, depending on how you plan to bake it) into your bowl or a round container that matches its shape.

- **5. Cold Retard (Overnight):**
    - Yes this is what it's called (also cold proof - same thing)
    - Place the covered bowl straight into the refrigerator for **12 to 24 hours** to deepen flavor and firm up the dough.
# Bake (Saturday)
- **6. Bake:**
    - Preheat your Dutch oven (with the lid on) inside your oven at **450°F (230°C)** for 45 to 60 minutes.    
    - Turn your chilled dough out onto a piece of parchment paper and score a deep slash across the top with a razor blade or sharp knife.
	    - **Scoring:**  first, make a light score to create 4 quadrants. Then add any designs to each quadrant, then go over the scores again and make them deeper.
	    - [See here for scoring techniques](https://breadtopia.com/how-to-score-dough/)
    - Carefully transfer the dough (using the parchment paper as a sling) into the hot Dutch oven, put the lid on, and bake for **20 minutes**.
	-  Remove the lid, *drop the oven temperature to 425°F (220°C)*, and bake for another *25 minutes* until deeply golden.
		- Start checking at 20min. 
	- **Cool**
	    - Cool completely on a *wire rack without parchment paper* for at least 1 hour before slicing!
# Storing The Loaf
**Days 1 to 3 (On the Counter):** Store the loaf cut-side down on a wooden cutting board, or slip it loosely into a paper bag, a clean cotton/linen kitchen towel, or a dedicated bread bag. This lets the bread breathe so the crust stays crunchy while keeping the exposed crumb from drying out too fast.

 **Days 4+ (Slice and Freeze)**: Once you've made your way through the first few days, slice up the remainder of the loaf, put the slices in a freezer-safe bag or container, and pop them straight into the freezer.
